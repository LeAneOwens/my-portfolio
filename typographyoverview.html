Typography Overview

Typography is more than merely a font. It is a type of communication that conveys the overall feel and readability of text. Proper typography is aethetically appealing and can be read with ease. This can draw attention to key content and enhance clarity. Typography is not only dependent on font choice, but also other elements such as alightnment and hierarchy. For example, the title of a article would be in larger font than the body. The art of typography is one of the basic foundations of graphic design and helps to harmoize text with the overall design elements. In essence, typography is the art of arranging type to make written language both readably and visually appealing.

Typography Terminology and Definitions

Contrast
The juxtaposition of different text elements to break up visual monotony and make text stand out from the background to improve readability.
Hierarchy
Variation of elements such as size, weight, and even color to stragetically call attention to text based on visual importance. For example, titles and heading should be distinct to guide users.
Spacing
The distance between and around characters, words, and lines to prevent visual clutter.
Alightment
How text and elements line up along a column, page, or grid to guide the reader.
Font
Not to be confused with typeface, font specific weight, size, and style within a typeface family. It is, in essence, the form that a typeface takes. For example, Calibri is a typeface. Calibri Bold is the font.
Typeface
Predating computers, a typeface is a set of characters with a common design theme such as Arial or Chiller.
Serif
Serif typefaces are characterized by their small decorative lines or "feet" at the ends of characters. They find a particular niche in text-heavy layouts such as books and newspapers.
Sans Serif
These typefaces are distinquished by their simplistic, ungilded style without the Serif's characterizing "feet". Sans Serifs provide a minimalistic look and have the advantage of being versatile in use.
Script
Typefaces that imitate the personal and natural aesthetic of handwriting or calligraphy. They can vary from casual to formal in style. They are ill-suited for body text.
Monospaced
Typefaces that were historically used in typewriters and coding envirnments. These typefaces are defined by characters that occupy the same horizontal space for superior uniformity.
