Typography Article

While the color themes were largely explored in the article on Color Theory, the site for Shadowlands, Jones Shadowlands Festival of Fear also makes fantastic use of typography. The typefaces and fonts used throughout the site are perfectly designed to conjure an eerie mood. The typeface is not unlike what you might find on cover of horror movies and novels. The spooky, alone-in-the-dark tone is conveyed to perfection with creativity and minimal use of vibrant colors. This ensures the sparse use of red is more impactful in its execution.

The typography is darksome and playfully grim in its whimsy in the first image and solemnly forboding in the second image. Both images showcase how use of typography in harmony with a well-thought out color scheme bring out the desired message of a design. The message here is that you are wandering alone in the dark of night in a haunted, isolated, spooky corn maze. It wants to send a chill up your spine. The red of the Festival of Fear wants to come across as a slasher teasing his prey rather than bring forth any warmth or cheerfulness despite the vibrancy of the hue. It's meant to be shockingly stark against the otherwise monotone feel, like a jump scare. Also take note of the following image:

While Shadowlands is like the blurred vision of one so scared and shook they've gone mad in their fear, the second attraction, Hysteria, is a walk through a cold, dark forest with a lurking eldrich stalker just beyond one's vison. It's those dreaded steps of trying to stay calm just before paranoia makes one break into a run. It's jagged like muscles jolting from panic and pale, icy cold like a phantom's breath chilling the back of your neck.

MythFolks, by comparison, is lackluster in its execution of typography. The typefaces do not grab attention as effectively and are rather dull despite the cryptic tone of the site's subject matter. The typeface is barebones with no flare such as the below image.

Instead of a feel like an old storybook or even a whimsical, but dignified typeface, the text is nondescript and monotone. When the look of the typeface does vary, it's not particularly impressive or worthy of note:
It's just a spiral in place of the 'o'. It's a bit playful, but for the most part it's just there. There's nothing impressively unique or memorable to really give it an impact. While the site has readability, it has no strong personality or sense of presence.
