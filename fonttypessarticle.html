Font Types Article

Most people have used played around with fonts in a word processor at some point in their life. For those who went on to dabble in design work, distinguishing between the different types of fonts and their ideal uses became an essential skill. There are five main types of fonts: Serif, Sans Serif, Decorative, Script, and Monospaced. Each of these have different advantages and strengths. When these strengths are kept in mind, they bring out the best in design work and text-heavy subjects.

For example, Serif typefaces are best used in text-laden items such as books, newspapers, and other forms of media that require lots of reading. The characteristic "feet" of the end strokes allow the eyes to naturally and fluidly glide across lines of text with clarity and minimal strain. Examples of such fonts include Goudy Old Style, which can be downloaded here. Serif fonts are perfect for providing formal, easy to read text. They are a common, basic style of typeface with no frills attached.

Sans Serif fonts are often found on digital screens. Similar to Serifs, they are straightforward and ungilded. They are a popular choice for webdesign and other digital media. They differ from Serifs in their lack of "feet". Fonts of this style are versatile, easy to read, and simplistic in style. Examples of Sans Serif fonts include Arial or Calibri, which can be found at here.

For a more nexpressive or even calligraphic font, Script is best practice. These fonts are often utilized in invitations, branding, and decorative situations where a more personal touch is sought after rather than the nondescript mood of Serifs or similar fonts. They can vary in style from casal and playful to fanciful and formal. Example of a Script font would be Brush Script MT or Gabriola, which can be found at here.

Monospaced fonts were originally used in typewriters and early computing. Coding environments now favor these typefaces as well as technical documents. A key characterisic of this font is that each character occupies the same horizonal space in a uniform, evenly spaced style. An example of Monospaced fonts would be Consolas, found here.

Lastly, there's Decorative fonts that are best used sparingly. Like Script, they can be hard to read if overused. They shine best when incorporated in logos, posters, or whereever artistic creativity is desired. They add a unique flare to creative work when properly used. Examples of Decorative fonts
