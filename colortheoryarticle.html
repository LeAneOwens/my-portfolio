Color Theory Article

An example of color theory and how it can be used to convey emotions and enhance the desired theme of a design can be found on the website Jones Shadowlands Festival of Fear. The use of colors on the site perfectly convey the spooky feel of wandering in the nebulous darkness of a haunted cornfield teeming with lurking frights just out of site. The overall color scheme of the site is very conservative with vibrancy and even evokes the feel of older slasher movies. For example:

Notice how toned down the hues are overall. The feel is mean to summon suspense and eerieness. As a haunted attraction, they want to inspire an atmosphere of creeping, quiet, lurking-just-out-of-sight horror similar to classic horror movies before the monster or slasher appears. It's the silence just before the monster or slasher jumps out after stalking just out of your vision. When vibrant colors are used such as the red of the Festival of Fear or the reds and purples of the below image, they do not inspire warmth and playfulness, but rather dramatic effect like the aftermath of the slasher's attack after they catch you off guard.

The feel of the entire site is mostly monochromatic and muted, making the rare use of bright color more striking and shocking, increasing the color scheme's overall impact.

In comparison this site on myths and folklore, MythFolks, falls a bit short of achieving as impactful of an effect. It lacks a mysterious vibe that could otherwise serve as the equivalent of Shadowlands' unsettling and nightmare-inspiring theme. What color is present on the site is minimal and not as cohesive.
